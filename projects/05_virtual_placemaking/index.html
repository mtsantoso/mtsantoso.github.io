Generative AI, VR & User Agency

Driving Long-Term Retention through Co-Creation and Collective Ownership for Small Teams

Generative AI, VR & User Agency: Driving Long-Term Retention through Co-Creation and Collective Ownership for Small Teams

Role: Experimental Design & Data Collection
Goal: Investigating how self-authored VR spaces and shared memories influence group dynamics

Project Summary

This longitudinal exploratory study (N=136) examined the psychological and behavioral outcomes of Virtual Placemaking. We investigated two variables: the impact of revisiting shared memories vs. envisioning plausible ones, and the impact of interacting in self-built vs. researcher-built virtual environments. Results demonstrated that self-built environments and revisiting shared pasts significantly increased group cohesion and collective psychological ownership.

The Challenge

The Social Problem: Remote collaboration and virtual social interactions often lack the sense of place and social presence found in physical environments, leading to lower group cohesion.

User Needs: Users in virtual environments need a sense of agency and shared history to feel connected to a group and a digital space.

Technical Constraints: Implementing a system where non-technical users could easily build a virtual environment while maintaining a consistent experimental framework across dozens of groups.

KPIs: Measuring long-term group cohesion and collective psychological ownership as precursors to sustainable product adoption in the metaverse.

Solution

We designed a 2×2 longitudinal experiment (two sessions, one week apart) to isolate the effects of agency and memory.

  • Memory Manipulation (Revisit vs. Envision): Half the groups recorded a real-world interaction reconstructed in VR to revisit. The other half envisioned a plausible future interaction in a similar VR setting.
  • Agency Manipulation (Self-Built vs. Others-Built): Half the groups used Virtual Placemaking tools to build their own environment (selecting assets, arranging furniture), while the other half entered a pre-made researcher-designed environment.
  • Multimodal Measurement: Self-report scales (Group Cohesion, Social Presence), behavioral tasks (Investment Task to measure collective ownership), and 6DOF motion tracking to measure interpersonal distance and synchronization.

Key Insights

1. Users who have a hand in creating their digital environment feel more connected to the group and the platform.

2. Revisiting past interactions in VR is a stronger driver of cohesion than imagining new ones.

3. Feeling that a space is “ours” (Collective Ownership) is distinct from simply feeling “there” (Social Presence).

4. Participants in self-built environments reported significantly higher collective psychological ownership and liking of the virtual space compared to those in environments built by others.

5. Revisiting shared memories led to greater increases in group cohesion and social presence than envisioning plausible future interactions.

Design Opportunities

  • Prioritize Co-Creation Tools. Allow users to build their shared AI-hub rather than providing static templates — this drives long-term retention and collective ownership.
  • Measure Success at the Group Level. Collaborative AI success should be measured by group cohesion metrics, not just individual user satisfaction. Design features that reward group-level milestones and shared placemaking activities.
  • Implement Interaction Recaps. Allow small teams to revisit the highlights of their last collaboration session, reinforcing shared memory and group identity.
